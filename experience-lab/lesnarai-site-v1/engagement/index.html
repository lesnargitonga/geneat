How we work

A project, from
first message to running.

Five stages. Each one ends with something you can hold rather than a status update, and nothing moves to the next until the last has actually landed.

  1. 01

    A first conversation

    You describe the problem rather than the build. We say what can be measured, what cannot, and what we would refuse to claim.

    You get: A free first conversation, no commitment

  2. 02

    Scope and shape

    We work out what has to exist, what it must never get wrong, and what would count as working. That decides the architecture more than a feature list does.

    You get: A written scope and a price

  3. 03

    Build

    Interface, backend, database and deployment. You see it running on its own service and domain, not as a repository handed over at the end.

    You get: Working software, deployed

  4. 04

    Prove it

    Reachability, health and failure isolation are tested rather than asserted, and the results are written down including what they do not prove.

    You get: Evidence you can re-run

  5. 05

    Run it, or hand it over

    We keep operating it, or hand it across with the infrastructure documented. Either way it stays yours.

    You get: Your choice, stated up front

What it costs

Priced per project, after stage two. A marketplace with payments and a merchant back office is not the same job as an embedded controller, and a rate card that pretends otherwise would be the first thing on this site you could not check.

You get a written scope and a fixed price before any build starts. If the scope changes, the price is re-agreed before the work happens, not after.

What we take on

  • Products people buy from - marketplaces, storefronts, ordering, payments
  • Software that runs an organisation - members, approvals, records, scheduling
  • AI held as inspectable structure rather than instructions in a prompt
  • Infrastructure, hardening and reliability work on Linux servers
  • Embedded control for equipment that has to keep running without a network
