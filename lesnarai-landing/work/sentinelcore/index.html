SentinelCore

A self-hostable security operations platform. It observes what you own, joins signals into incidents, acts only under governance, proves recovery independently and keeps the lessons. It holds every claim to evidence, its own included.

One protection loop

Twelve capabilities, each with one role in a single loop. Engine, alert and incident outputs feed the unified incident as evidence instead of running as parallel systems.

  1. Observe

    • Telemetry and owned-source collectionnormalises authorised observations
    • Assessment and engine adaptersbounded findings, kept as evidence

  2. Understand

    • Asset, exposure and identity contextwho owns what, and what is exposed
    • Intelligence, correlation and attributionrelationships, with their confidence

  3. Act

    • Governed responsea graduated policy ladder; live actions are recommendations
    • Authorised adversary validationscoped testing, isolated from protection authority
    • Control plane and runtimecoordinates bounded work; orchestration is not authority

  4. Recover

    • Recovery verificationrestoration proven independently, not assumed

  5. Learn

    • Assurance evidence and institutional learningtimelines, outcomes and lessons retained
    • Reality Labcontrolled scenarios, kept apart from observed evidence

Real engines, pinned and verified

SentinelCore drives established scanners rather than imitating them. Each engine is pinned to a version and SHA-verified before it runs, under bounded runtime controls. Every engine has run against owned canaries in five categories: DNS and TLS, repositories, container images, infrastructure as code, and hosts.

Container-image scanning runs without a Docker socket: OCI archive in, real vulnerabilities out.

Detection, measured honestly

A purple-team baseline catalogues 28 techniques across 10 ATT&CK tactics, including deserialisation, XXE, dependency confusion, prompt injection, OAuth pre-account hijack and TLS downgrade. Every one fires a detection. Only the ones that show an active intrusion are correlated into an incident. Posture findings such as missing headers stay visible as findings, and are never relabelled as attacks.

Containment that can be undone

Live response is recommendation-only. Containment is rehearsed on synthetic and canary targets under a governed lifecycle.

  1. Exact authorisationSingle-use, exact-target permission, enforced again by a separate authenticated action broker.
  2. Durable leasesEvery action holds a lease and a hash-chained state, from proposed to contained to recovered.
  3. Reversible actionsA synthetic identity session revoked and restored; a labelled workload disconnected and reconnected, with unrelated resources unchanged.
  4. Failure gatesStale, duplicate, replayed, concurrent, partial-failure, restart, cross-tenant and emergency-stop cases all pass.

Decisions qualified blind

The decision engine is deterministic and auditable. Language models may enrich evidence for a person; they do not decide.

Its first blind qualification failed. The record keeps that failure, and retracts the "generalisation proven" claims made by test packs the same process had written. Independent packs moved accuracy from 0.867 to 0.967. Detection precision (0.933, one suspicious case escalated to an incident) is still under its 0.95 gate, so the engine has no production authority. A fine-tuned language model failed the same kind of test (accuracy 0.917, 21 grounding failures). That is why it enriches evidence and never makes the call.

Attack, detect, decide, contain

A governed purple-team lab attacks owned loopback clones only, never a live deployment.

  1. Seven techniquesPath traversal, SQL injection, command injection, IDOR, SSRF, template injection and authentication bypass, each confirmed by the response it produced.
  2. Into the loopEach confirmed exploit becomes a protection signal, then a governed incident, then a contained and recovered target, findings and an open remediation case.
  3. CampaignsThree multi-stage campaigns, all three contained before data left.
  4. A gap it foundExploitation chains that reached data were only rated suspicious. A principled exfiltration trigger now makes them incidents, while approved bulk access stays benign.

What this page does not show

  • A customer deployment: there is none. Deployment readiness is recorded with gaps still open.
  • Scale: it is a single-node, self-hostable platform, not a distributed EDR or SIEM.
  • A malware sandbox: isolation today is container-based, not a microVM.
  • Live response: actions on real systems are recommendations a person approves.
  • Offensive testing outside the lab: only against owned or written-authorised targets.
  • Targets, findings or clients: never published.
