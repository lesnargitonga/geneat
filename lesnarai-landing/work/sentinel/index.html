Work / Autonomous systems

Operation Sentinel

An autonomy stack that flies a PX4 aircraft through a mission, stops for what is really there, and answers to an operator. Built and proven in simulation.

Prototype PX4/Gazebo software-in-the-loop · no physical flight

What the film shows, shot by shot
  1. 0–6 s A reconstruction: the x500 model, the run's world, the recorded descent. An explanatory rendering, not flight evidence.
  2. 6–10 s The real simulation frame separates into the layers it is made of.
  3. 10–17 s The stack a mission runs on: Gazebo world, PX4 SITL, the bridge, telemetry, the operator system.
  4. 17–21 s The operator surface, replaying the sealed run's telemetry.
  5. 21–28 s The explanation collapses back into the real frame, then black.

Four parts are ours. The aircraft, the autopilot and the world are not.

Autonomy bridge

Turns a mission into offboard velocity commands over MAVSDK, and refuses to navigate until PX4 has actually accepted offboard control.

Mission logic

Waypoints, an A* grid and an avoidance field built from the world's real geometry, with a hard stop at the lidar.

Telemetry pipeline

Position, attitude, commands, the full 72-ray scan, battery and mission state, about five times a second, sealed with the run.

Operator console

Dispatches the mission, shows it live, and reports its link to the backend as measured rather than assumed.

Upstream, and credited: PX4 Autopilot flies the aircraft, Gazebo simulates the world, and MAVSDK carries the commands.

One mission, end to end

  1. IdeaFly a mission with no cloud dependency in flight, and eventually without GPS.
  2. ObserveA 72-ray lidar scan and the PX4 estimator.
  3. Model180 obstacles read from the world file the run flew in.
  4. EngineerWaypoints become offboard velocity commands, steered by the grid and the avoidance field.
  5. ProtectA lidar hard stop, a heading guard, and an abort if offboard control cannot be engaged.
  6. Human reviewAn operator dispatches the mission and watches it run.
  7. ActPX4 flies the x500 in Gazebo.
  8. ProveThe run is sealed: a manifest with a SHA-256 for every file, the mission, the bridge log, the telemetry and the world.

7 sealed runs. The first two failed.

Every run flew the console's training mission: a 25 m box at 10 m. Two timed out after 631 s. After two fixes, 5 of 5 completed, in 126–216 s. Each run number opens its sealed record on GitHub.

The drone stopped for a wall that was not there.

Run 1 never left its hover. The bridge asked PX4 for offboard control in a gap between setpoints, PX4 refused, and the bridge carried on as if it were navigating. It now sends a setpoint before every request, retries, and aborts the mission if control cannot be engaged.

Run 2 flew the north leg, turned east, and stopped 1.6 m from an obstacle. It held there for 8.8 minutes.

The simulated lidar modelled every box as a disc half as wide as its longest side. Wall_1, 1 m thick and 38 m long, became a 19 m disc reaching into the flight path. The real wall was 20.7 m away, beyond the sensor's range.

The fix intersects each ray with each obstacle in that obstacle's own frame, rotation included. It agreed with brute-force ray marching on 3,000 random shapes before it flew. Every run since has completed.

Before the missions: 87 perception runs

GPS-denied flight is configured, not yet shown.

The estimator carries the parameters for flying without satellites. The sealed runs fly with simulated GPS and a compass. Flying the same mission with GPS fusion off is the next proof, not a result.

  • EKF2_GPS_CTRL GPS fusion disabled
  • EKF2_AID_MASK optical-flow aiding
  • EKF2_HGT_MODE range sensor for height

What this page does not show

  • Any physical aircraft. None has been flown.
  • A field test, a range or an endurance figure.
  • More than one aircraft at a time.
  • Height in the lidar model: the scan is flat.
  • Flight evidence in the film's opening or its layered space; both are explanatory renderings, and its operator surface is a recorded replay.
